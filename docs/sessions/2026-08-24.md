# 2026-08-24 — S2: the nasty-CSV fixture corpus

## What changed

- `tests/fixtures/nasty/` created: 15 CSVs, one failure mode each, plus
  `MANIFEST.md` (239 lines) specifying for every fixture what is wrong, what a
  correct parse produces, and what the parser must never do silently.
- No `src/` code touched. This session builds the adversary only, per S2.

## Verified, not assumed

Ran `pandas.read_csv` with defaults over the whole corpus. 5 of 15 raise
(`01`, `04`, `05`, `12`, `15`); the other 10 "succeed" while being wrong, which
is the point of the corpus. Two manifest claims were corrected after checking
the real behaviour rather than shipping a plausible-sounding one (I8):

- **03 (BOM):** pandas' C engine strips the BOM. The failure is one layer up —
  `open(..., encoding='utf-8')` and `csv.reader`, which the delimiter sniffer and
  header detector will use, return `'﻿customer_key'`. Manifest now says so.
- **09 (nulls):** `None` *is* in pandas' `STR_NA_VALUES`, so the deliberate trap
  fires — 4 of 10 real `addon_tier` categories become NaN silently. `-`, `--`
  and `?` are *not* defaults, so they survive as strings and hold
  `support_calls` at object dtype. Both directions are now documented.

Excel serial dates in `07` were computed against the 1899-12-30 origin and the
expected values in the manifest are the computed ones.
